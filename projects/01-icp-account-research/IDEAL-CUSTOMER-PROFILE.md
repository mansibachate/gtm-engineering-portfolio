# Ideal Customer Profile: Clay

> Built October 2026 · Perspective: a BDR selling Clay, the GTM data and enrichment platform
> Sources are public (vendor pages, reviews, press). Figures marked *(est.)* are my estimates; figures marked *(reported)* come from secondhand sources and are unverified.

## ICP summary

Clay's ideal customer is a **B2B company with an active outbound motion and at least one person who owns sales operations or growth**, typically 50–1,000 employees, Series A through late-stage, selling software or services to other businesses.

These teams already have a CRM and a sequencing tool but are unhappy with their data: single-provider email coverage is low, lists are generic, and reps spend hours on manual research. They want to act on buying signals (funding, hiring, job changes) but have no system to do it.

The strongest single signal is **hiring for GTM engineering, RevOps, or growth ops roles.** It means the company has decided to build systems, and Clay is the most common tool those people use.

## 1. Firmographics

| Criteria | Ideal range | Why it matters | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employees | 50–1,000 (core); 10–50 for self-serve | Big enough to run outbound at volume, small enough to move fast | < 10 with no outbound motion |
| Revenue | $5M–$200M ARR *(est.)* | Can afford a few thousand to tens of thousands of dollars a year on GTM tooling | Pre-revenue with no sales team |
| Industry | B2B SaaS, AI/devtools, fintech, GTM agencies, recruiting & staffing, VC/PE (deal sourcing) | All depend on finding and contacting the right companies | B2C, retail, local services |
| Geography | North America, UK, Western Europe | Data coverage is strongest; English-language outbound | Markets with thin data coverage |
| Stage | Series A–D and growth | Funded, with pressure to grow pipeline | Very late-stage firms with an in-house data team |
| Growth | Headcount growing 20%+ a year; raised in the last 12 months | New money brings new pipeline targets | Layoffs or a hiring freeze in sales |

## 2. Technographics

- **CRM:** Salesforce or HubSpot (required: Clay pushes data into it)
- **Sequencer:** Outreach, Salesloft, Instantly, Smartlead, or Lemlist
- **Data tools already in use:** Apollo, ZoomInfo, Lusha, LinkedIn Sales Navigator. They have a data budget, and Clay can consolidate or augment it.
- **Sophistication:** 3–4 out of 5. They need someone comfortable with spreadsheets, formulas, and basic APIs. Too low and nobody can build; too high and they may have built pipelines in-house.
- **Strong fit signal:** Job descriptions that mention Clay, n8n, or "enrichment workflows"

## 3. Buying signals (why now)

| Signal | Why it matters | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring a GTM Engineer, RevOps, or Growth Ops role | They've decided to build systems | LinkedIn Jobs, careers pages |
| Hiring several SDRs/BDRs at once | Scaling outbound; data quality will break | Job boards |
| New VP Sales, CRO, or Head of Growth (< 90 days) | New leaders review and replace the stack | LinkedIn job changes, press releases |
| Raised funding in the last 6 months | Budget plus pipeline pressure | Crunchbase, TechCrunch |
| ZoomInfo or Apollo renewal coming up | A natural moment to re-evaluate data spend | Discovery calls, review sites |
| Launching into a new segment or region | Needs new lists fast | Press releases, blog, LinkedIn posts |

## 4. Pain points

| # | Pain | Severity | How it shows up | Clay's angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Low data coverage** | Critical | Bounced emails; "we can't find emails for half our list" | Waterfall enrichment across 50+ providers |
| 2 | **Generic outbound, low replies** | High | Reply rates under 1–2%; every email looks the same | Signals and AI research make every message specific |
| 3 | **Manual research eats rep time** | High | SDRs spend hours per day on LinkedIn and Google | Automate research; reps only work qualified accounts |
| 4 | **Expensive, seat-based data contracts** | Medium–High | Paying for ZoomInfo seats that aren't fully used | Pay by usage; combine cheaper providers |
| 5 | **Can't act on signals** | Medium | "We know funding and hiring matter, but no one tracks them" | Signal monitoring feeding straight into the CRM and sequences |

## 5. Budget

- **Self-serve plans** *(reported, Oct 2026)*: Free; Launch from ~$185/mo; Growth from ~$495/mo; Enterprise is custom-priced. That's about **$2K–$6K+ a year** before credit top-ups.
- **Enterprise deals:** likely **$25K–$100K+ a year** *(est.)*, depending on usage
- **Who holds the budget:** VP Sales, CRO, or Head of RevOps; the founder at smaller companies
- **Price objection to expect:** credit-based pricing is hard to predict, a common complaint in reviews. Prepare a cost-per-record example.

## 6. Buyer personas

### The RevOps / GTM Engineering lead: *user and champion*
- **Goals:** clean data, faster workflows, more pipeline per rep
- **Pain, in their words:** "I'm duct-taping five tools together and the data's still bad."
- **What resonates:** concrete workflows, templates, coverage numbers, community examples
- **Turn-offs:** fluffy ROI claims, being sold to like an executive
- **Opening line:** *"Saw you're hiring a GTM engineer. Usually that's when teams outgrow single-source enrichment. What's your current coverage rate?"*

### The VP Sales / CRO: *economic buyer*
- **Goals:** hit the pipeline number, make reps productive, control costs
- **Pain:** "We added reps but pipeline didn't grow with them."
- **What resonates:** pipeline generated, meetings per rep, consolidating data spend
- **Objections:** "We already pay for ZoomInfo." / "Who's going to build this?"
- **Opening line:** *"Congrats on the new role. Most new CROs I talk to look at data spend vs. pipeline in the first 90 days. Worth comparing notes?"*

### The founder (10–50 employees): *does it all*
- **Goals:** first repeatable outbound motion, without hiring a big SDR team
- **What resonates:** speed, self-serve, "one person can run outbound like a team of five"

## 7. Negative ICP (who not to sell to)

1. B2C companies, which sell to consumers rather than businesses
2. Teams under 10 people with no one doing outbound
3. No CRM at all: they need basics first
4. Companies with a mature in-house data engineering team that has built its own pipelines
5. Sales teams that only do inbound and have no outbound plans
6. Highly regulated buyers with strict limits on third-party data (some government, healthcare)
7. A recent multi-year lock-in with a competitor (wait for the renewal window)
8. Layoffs or freezes in sales within the last 6 months

## 8. Scoring rubric (100 points)

| Category | Points | Full points when… |
|---|---|---|
| Firmographic fit | 25 | B2B, 50–1,000 employees, target industry and region, funded |
| Technographic fit | 15 | Has a CRM and sequencer, plus an existing data tool |
| Pain alignment | 20 | Clear evidence of outbound at scale with data problems |
| Budget capacity | 20 | Funded or profitable, already paying for data tools |
| Contact access | 10 | RevOps/GTM lead and VP Sales identified |
| Timing signal | 10 | At least one signal from section 3 in the last 90 days |

**Grades:** A (75–100) pursue now · B (60–74) pursue, light personalization · C (40–59) nurture · D (< 40) skip

**60-second check:** B2B? 50–1,000 employees? Outbound team? A timing signal? Can you name the RevOps or sales leader? (5 yes ≈ A, 3–4 ≈ B, 1–2 ≈ C)

## 9. Prospecting playbook

- **Best list to start with:** companies with open GTM Engineer, RevOps, or SDR roles. On LinkedIn Jobs, search `"GTM Engineer" OR "Revenue Operations"`, filtered to companies with 51–1,000 employees.
- **Sales Navigator filters:** Company headcount 51–1,000 · Industry: Software, Financial Services, Staffing · Title: RevOps, Growth, Sales Ops, VP Sales · "Changed jobs in the last 90 days"
- **Before outreach, check:** CRM (from job posts or BuiltWith), current data tool, recent funding, open sales roles, and whether a RevOps person exists
- **Disqualify fast:** B2C? Under 10 employees? No sales team? Skip.

## 10. Competitive context

| Competitor | Their position | How Clay wins |
|---|---|---|
| ZoomInfo | Largest database; enterprise, seat-based pricing | Many sources instead of one; usage-based; more flexible workflows |
| Apollo | All-in-one database plus sequencing for SMBs | Better coverage through waterfalls; deeper customization |
| Cognism, Lusha | Contact data, with Cognism strong in Europe | Clay can *use* them as providers rather than replace them |
| AI coding agents + open source (Claude Code, n8n) | Teams build their own pipelines | Faster to set up, no maintenance, 50+ integrations ready-made |

**Positioning statement:** *Clay helps GTM teams turn every data source and buying signal into pipeline, without being locked into one database.*

**Trend to watch:** AI coding agents make it easier for technical teams to build their own enrichment pipelines. This raises Clay's bar with highly technical teams and makes the RevOps-led, mid-market segment even more important.
